The short version

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking across other apps or websites, and no selling of data.
  • The game keeps almost everything on your phone. Your scores, stats, settings and progress stay on your phone unless you post a ranked score.
  • If you post ranked scores, we keep your public display name, your scores and the replays that check them, on our server. You can remove them at any time with "Remove my scores" in the Game, or by email.
  • Short Final is for players aged 13 and up. A player under 13 goes no further than the age screen. See "Children" below.
  • The game never asks for your location, contacts, photos or anything else on your phone.

Who we are and how to reach us

Thin Air Studios LLC, Colorado, USA. Email: thinaircapital@gmail.com. This policy covers the Short Final app for Android, the online leaderboards and name checks, and the Short Final website.

What stays on your phone

  • Your scores, stars, stats for each airport and level, achievements, settings, streaks and the airports you played recently.
  • Airport maps and weather downloaded for the airports you open. They are kept so they open quickly next time.
  • The flight route files the game downloads for the airlines it has seen. They are kept for up to a week (or until the game needs the room), then fetched again when needed.
  • A video you make to share, until you make another or close the share panel without sharing or saving it (see Sharing).
  • The picture of the result you last shared or saved. It stays in the game's own storage until you share or save another picture, and it is removed when you uninstall the game.
  • Your answer to the age question: the year you were born, and the month too when the year alone does not tell whether you are 13 yet. It is stored only on your phone, and it is never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Whether you have agreed to the Terms of Use and this policy, and the version and date you agreed.
  • How many free games you have started on each day, and whether Short Final Pro is unlocked on this phone, as Google Play last told the game (see Purchases).

None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the game, or clearing the game's storage in Android's settings, deletes everything the game keeps on your phone, including your answer to the age question. Choosing "Reset progress" in Settings deletes your scores, stats, achievements, streaks and your agreement to the Terms of Use and this policy, so the game asks you to agree again. Your settings and your answer to the age question stay, and so does your sign-in, so "Remove my scores" still removes any scores you posted before. A picture you save to the Pictures folder and a video you save to the Movies folder stay in those folders after you uninstall the game, so delete them there if you want them gone.

Internet requests the game makes

To draw an airport and fill its sky, the game asks public services for information about the airport you choose, and about the real planes near it. These requests contain the airport's code or coordinates. They don't contain a flight's callsign, your location or anything about you.

  • Our website (thin-air-studios.github.io), for an airport's map: its layout, coastline, water and peaks. The request names the airport's code (such as KDFW.json), and the file is the same for every player. The game asks for it before it asks the servers below. A map the game gets is kept on your phone for up to 30 days.
  • OpenStreetMap data for the airport's map, coastline, water and peaks, from the public Overpass API servers: overpass.kumi.systems and overpass-api.de. The game asks them only when our website has no map for the airport. The 50 busiest airports, Midland (KMAF) and Lukla (VNLK) ship inside the game and need no download. See the OpenStreetMap privacy policy.
  • The OpenSky Network for the live feed near the airport. See opensky-network.org. Each time you open the game, come back to it after 10 minutes or more, when the connection comes back after the phone was offline, or tap the live feed line on the home screen, the game makes one check with the OpenSky Network to see whether the live feed can be reached. The check asks for flights around the airport you last played, or today's airport, the same way a live game does. It doesn't use your location.
  • aviationweather.gov, run by the US National Weather Service's Aviation Weather Center, for the airport's weather report. See the NOAA privacy policy.
  • Our website (thin-air-studios.github.io), for the flight route files, so the game can say which city a real plane arrived from. The files are one small list for each airline, with the routes its flights fly. When a real plane appears near the airport, the game fetches its airline's file the first time it needs it, and keeps the file on your phone for up to a week (or until the game needs the room). The request asks for the airline's file (such as AAL.json). It does not name a flight, the airport or your location. For the largest airlines the file is split into one part for each first digit of the flight number, and the request then asks for that part (such as AAL-1.json), which names no flight, the airport or your location either. The game asks once for each airline, and the file is the same for every player.

The game also makes one request that is not about an airport or a flight:

  • Our server, once a day, to see whether the online boards are open. The request carries no name, score or identifier.

When the game asks the services above for flights, weather, maps or route files, they see your phone's internet address (IP address), and nothing else about you. Our server sees the address for the request above, and nothing else about you either. Their own policies apply to what they do with it.

Our website is hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub sees the internet address of each request for a map or a route file, as any website's host does, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Online leaderboards and ranked games

Ranked games can be posted to the online World boards and to Google Play Games. Viewing the boards needs no sign-in. Posting needs Google Play Games sign-in.

What we store for a score posted to the online boards:

  • Your Google Play Games player ID, which we use to tell players apart. We don't see your Google password or your Google account details.
  • Your display name, as Google Play Games shows it. If you turn off "Show my name on World boards", the board shows a callsign such as "Pilot 4821" instead.
  • The score, the airport, the level, whether it was a daily challenge game, the date and time, and the other results shown on the board (for example passengers landed).
  • The replay of the game: the information needed to play it again exactly, such as the traffic and weather it was played with and the moves you made. We use the replay to check the score.

Who can see it. Scores and names on the World boards are public. Anyone who opens the boards can see them. Google Play Games boards are public inside Google Play Games, under Google's rules. Scores posted to Google Play Games are sent by the game to Google, not to us. We don't receive their replays and we don't check them, so a Google Play Games board can show a score that the World boards did not accept.

How long we keep it:

  • Scores stay on the boards until you remove them, or until the board's season closes. Seasons can change when the rules for ranked play change. When a season closes, its scores leave the boards, but we keep them in our database.
  • Replays are kept only for the top 100 scores on each board. The replays of other scores are deleted once the score has been checked.
  • "Remove my scores" deletes your scores, their replays, your entries on the boards, the reports you filed, and your player record on our server: your Google Play Games player ID, your display name and your sign-in session. It also deletes the name check for your display name and the reports filed against that name, unless another player has the same name. It does not change the stats on your phone, and it does not remove scores already posted to Google Play Games.

Your choices. You can remove your scores at any time with "Remove my scores" in the Game, or by emailing thinaircapital@gmail.com with the subject "Remove my scores" and the display name you used. We will act on an email request within 30 days, and it removes the same things.

Name checks

Names on the online boards are checked to keep them appropriate.

  • The check first compares the name with lists of banned words, with allowances for normal aviation words. Names that fail this check are shown as a callsign.
  • A name that passes this check is sent to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI models, which decides whether the name is allowed. We send only the name text, not your player ID, your scores or your replays. Once Anthropic gives a decision we keep it, so the same name isn't sent again. If a check gets no decision (for example, because it fails or times out), the name is sent again on the player's next post or sign-in, until one does.
  • Anthropic handles the name under its own terms and privacy policy. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Players who are signed in and have posted ranked games can report a name in the game. After three reports from different players, the name is replaced with a callsign. Reports are linked to the reporting player's ID so that each player counts once.
  • Names are checked again when the name in Google Play Games changes.

Our server and database

The online boards run on Cloudflare Workers and a Cloudflare D1 database, on Cloudflare's global network. Cloudflare runs each request in the data centre that receives it, which may be outside the United States. Cloudflare processes the information described above on our behalf. Cloudflare's own network logs follow its privacy policy. Our server doesn't store your IP address alongside your scores or your name. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Purchases

Short Final Pro is a one-time purchase made through Google Play. Google Play takes the payment, and its payment details are never seen or stored by the game or by us. The game asks Google Play for the price, whether your Google account has bought Pro, and whether a purchase is still pending. It checks the answer on your phone and keeps it there, so Pro still works when you are offline. Your purchase is not sent to our server. Google keeps its own records of your purchases under its privacy policy.

Google Play Games

If you have Google Play Games on your phone, the game can sign you in to it, so your achievements and scores appear there. Google receives the information it needs to provide that service, including your Play Games identity, your display name and avatar, the achievements you unlock, and the scores you post. Google also collects diagnostics to keep the service working. Google handles this under its privacy policy.

Play Games sign-in starts only after you have answered the age question and agreed to the Terms of Use and this policy. Play Games is optional: the game plays without it.

Radio voices

The radio calls are spoken by your phone's own text-to-speech engine. The game passes that engine only the radio lines, such as "Speedbird 12, cleared to land runway 27 left". On many phones the engine is provided by Google, which handles the text under its own privacy policy. You can turn radio voices off in Settings.

Sharing

When you tap Share, the game makes a picture or text from your result and passes it to the app you choose. That app's own privacy policy then applies. Save image and Copy text are the exception: they keep the picture or the caption on your phone instead, as described below. Shared results contain your airport, score and game stats. They don't contain your name, location or account details. Nothing is shared unless you tap Share.

A video (the Video format on the end card) is made on your phone from the game's own record of the game you played. It shows your airport, your score and the real flights as the game shows them, with their names and flight numbers. Nothing is posted by the game. Share passes the video to the app you choose, and Save video keeps a copy in your phone's Movies folder. The video's file stays in the game's own storage on your phone until you make another video, or close the share panel without sharing or saving it.

Save image keeps a picture of your result in the Pictures folder of your phone, in a folder called Short Final. Copy text puts the caption on your phone's clipboard. For apps that do not show the caption a share sends, the caption is copied to the clipboard as well, so you can paste it. Neither one sends anything to us.

Every shared result names Short Final on Google Play. The link at the end of a caption goes to the game's Google Play page, and it carries an install referrer: the app you shared to (or "share" for the share sheet), the word "share" and the airport. Google Play reports that referrer to us with an install, so we can see which shares bring installs. It holds no name, score or daily number. Where an app does not make links tappable, such as TikTok or Instagram, the caption names the game on Google Play in words and has no link. Tapping a link opens Google Play, under Google's privacy policy.

A "Beat my score" link shared in an earlier version opens the game at that airport, through our website's page for it, thin-air-studios.github.io/play/. That link holds the airport, the daily number for a daily result and the app you shared to. If the phone has no game, that page passes the app's name and the airport to Google Play as the install referrer, and nothing else.

Children

Short Final is not for children under 13. The game is for players aged 13 and up, and a player under 13 cannot play it.

When a new player starts the game, it asks the year they were born. If that year alone does not tell whether the player is 13 yet (they turn 13 this calendar year), it also asks the month they were born. A player who is under 13 sees a screen that says Short Final is for ages 13 and up, and goes no further. There is no sign-in, no posting and no other part of the game for them. Until the age question is answered, the game sends nothing over the internet, so nothing about that player leaves the phone.

The answer stays on the phone, as described above. It is never sent to us or anyone else. Reset progress keeps it. Only clearing the game's storage in Android's settings, or uninstalling the game, removes it. It is not copied to Android's cloud backup or to a new phone, so after a restore the age question is asked again.

We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The game never asks for a name, email address or location. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, email thinaircapital@gmail.com and we will delete it. A parent or guardian who wants to know what we hold about their child, or to have it deleted, can ask us at the same address.

Where your information is stored

Information we hold for the online boards is stored by Cloudflare, in the location assigned to our database when it was created. Our service providers (Cloudflare, Anthropic and Google) may process it elsewhere under their own safeguards.

Security

We send information over encrypted connections (HTTPS). No system is perfectly secure, so we also keep only what the game needs.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to ask what information we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, or to object to how it is used. To use these rights, email thinaircapital@gmail.com. We will respond within the time the law requires. We don't sell personal information, and we don't use it for targeted advertising.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version here with a new effective date. If the change is material, the game will show it to you and ask you to agree again.
