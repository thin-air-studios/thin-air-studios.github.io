The short version

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking across other apps or websites, and no selling of data.
  • The game keeps almost everything on your phone. Your scores, stats, settings and progress stay on your phone unless you post a ranked score.
  • If you post ranked scores, we keep your public display name, your scores and the replays that check them, on our server. You can remove them at any time with "Remove my scores" in the Game, or by email.
  • Players under 13 can use a kid experience. We don't collect personal information from them. See "Children" below.
  • The game never asks for your location, contacts, photos or anything else on your phone.

What stays on your phone

  • Your scores, stars, stats for each airport and level, achievements, settings, streaks and the airports you played recently.
  • Airport maps and weather downloaded for the airports you open. They are kept so they open quickly next time.
  • Your answer to the birth-year question. It is stored only on your phone, and it is never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Whether you have agreed to the Terms of Use and this policy, and the version and date you agreed.

None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the game, or clearing the game's storage in Android's settings, deletes everything the game keeps on your phone. Choosing "Reset progress" in Settings deletes your scores, stats, achievements, streaks, your birth-year answer and your agreement to the Terms of Use and this policy, so the age question asks again. Your settings stay.

Internet requests the game makes

To draw an airport and fill its sky, the game asks public services for information about the airport you choose, and about the real planes near it. These requests contain the airport's code or coordinates, or a flight's callsign. They don't contain your location or anything about you.

  • OpenStreetMap data for the airport's map, coastline, water and peaks, from the public Overpass API servers: overpass.kumi.systems and overpass-api.de. The 50 busiest airports and Midland (KMAF) ship inside the game and need no download. See the OpenStreetMap privacy policy.
  • The OpenSky Network for the live feed near the airport. See opensky-network.org. Each time you open the game, come back to it after 10 minutes or more, or tap the live feed line on the home screen, the game makes one check with the OpenSky Network to see whether the live feed can be reached. The check asks for flights around the airport you last played, or today's airport, the same way a live game does. It doesn't use your location.
  • aviationweather.gov, run by the US National Weather Service's Aviation Weather Center, for the airport's weather report. See the NOAA privacy policy.
  • adsbdb (api.adsbdb.com), a public flight-route service, so the game can say which city a real plane arrived from. When a real plane appears near the airport, the game asks where that flight came from. The request holds only that flight's callsign (the flight number it broadcasts, such as BAW85U), not the airport and not your location. The game asks once for each flight, and keeps the answers in the game's memory until the app closes. Nothing from it is stored on your phone.

The game also makes two requests that are not about an airport or a flight:

  • Our server, once a day, to see whether the online boards are open. The request carries no name, score or identifier.
  • The Google Play store page, to see whether the game's store page is live, so a shared result can link to it. When a player taps Share, the game sends Google one request with no page content (a HEAD request to the store page's address). It stops once the page answers. For a player under 13 it is sent only after the grown-up question is answered (see "Children" below). See Google's privacy policy.

As with any internet request, these services can see your phone's internet address (IP address). The game doesn't send them anything else about you. Their own policies apply to what they do with it.

Online leaderboards and ranked games

Ranked games can be posted to the online World boards and to Google Play Games. Viewing the boards needs no sign-in. Posting needs Google Play Games sign-in.

What we store for a score posted to the online boards:

  • Your Google Play Games player ID, which we use to tell players apart. We don't see your Google password or your Google account details.
  • Your display name, as Google Play Games shows it. If you turn off "Show my name on World boards", the board shows a callsign such as "Pilot 4821" instead.
  • The score, the airport, the level, whether it was a daily challenge game, the date and time, and the other results shown on the board (for example passengers landed).
  • The replay of the game: the information needed to play it again exactly, such as the traffic and weather it was played with and the moves you made. We use the replay to check the score.

Who can see it. Scores and names on the World boards are public. Anyone who opens the boards can see them. Google Play Games boards are public inside Google Play Games, under Google's rules. Scores posted to Google Play Games are sent by the game to Google, not to us. We don't receive their replays and we don't check them, so a Google Play Games board can show a score that the World boards did not accept.

How long we keep it:

  • Scores stay on the boards until you remove them, or until the board's season closes. Seasons can change when the rules for ranked play change. When a season closes, its scores leave the boards, but we keep them in our database.
  • Replays are kept only for the top 100 scores on each board. The replays of other scores are deleted once the score has been checked.
  • "Remove my scores" deletes your scores, their replays, your entries on the boards, the reports you filed, and your player record on our server: your Google Play Games player ID, your display name and your sign-in session. It does not change the stats on your phone, and it does not remove scores already posted to Google Play Games.

Your choices. You can remove your scores at any time with "Remove my scores" in the Game, or by emailing thinaircapital@gmail.com with the subject "Remove my scores" and the display name you used. We will act on an email request within 30 days, and it removes the same things.

Name checks

Names on the online boards are checked to keep them appropriate.

  • The check first compares the name with lists of banned words, with allowances for normal aviation words. Names that fail this check are shown as a callsign.
  • A name that passes this check is sent to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI models, which decides whether the name is allowed. We send only the name text, not your player ID, your scores or your replays. Once Anthropic gives a decision we keep it, so the same name isn't sent again. If a check gets no decision (for example, because it fails or times out), the name is sent again on the player's next post or sign-in, until one does.
  • Anthropic handles the name under its own terms and privacy policy. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Players who are signed in and have posted ranked games can report a name in the game. After three reports from different players, the name is replaced with a callsign. Reports are linked to the reporting player's ID so that each player counts once.
  • Names are checked again when the name in Google Play Games changes.

Our server and database

The online boards run on Cloudflare Workers and a Cloudflare D1 database, on Cloudflare's global network. Cloudflare runs each request in the data centre that receives it, which may be outside the United States. Cloudflare processes the information described above on our behalf. Cloudflare's own network logs follow its privacy policy. Our server doesn't store your IP address alongside your scores or your name. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Google Play Games

If you have Google Play Games on your phone and are 13 or older, the game can sign you in to it, so your achievements and scores appear there. Google receives the information it needs to provide that service, including your Play Games identity, your display name and avatar, the achievements you unlock, and the scores you post. Google also collects diagnostics to keep the service working. Google handles this under its privacy policy.

Play Games sign-in is never started for a player in the kid experience (see "Children" below). Play Games is optional: the game plays without it.

Radio voices

The radio calls are spoken by your phone's own text-to-speech engine. The game passes that engine only the radio lines, such as "Speedbird 12, cleared to land runway 27 left". On many phones the engine is provided by Google, which handles the text under its own privacy policy. You can turn radio voices off in Settings.

Sharing

When you tap Share, the game makes a picture or text from your result and passes it to the app you choose. That app's own privacy policy then applies. Shared results contain your airport, score and game stats. They don't contain your name, location or account details. Nothing is shared unless you tap Share.

Children

The kid experience (players born 13 or fewer calendar years ago). The game asks each new player what year they were born. If the player was born 13 or fewer calendar years ago, the game runs in its kid experience. That includes every player under 13 and some 13-year-olds. In the kid experience:

  • The birth year stays on the phone and is never sent to us or anyone else. It can't be changed in Settings; only "Reset progress" asks again.
  • There is no Google Play Games sign-in, no posting of scores and no name on the online boards.
  • The game does not collect or store personal information about a player under 13, apart from the birth year above, which stays on the phone. The game never asks for a name, email address or location. Achievements and stats stay on the phone.
  • Players can look at the World boards. Names on them are filtered.
  • Before a kid shares, saves or copies a card or the feed report, or follows a web link or email, the game asks a grown-up to answer a simple question. The Google Play store-page check is part of that: it is sent only after the grown-up answers. Viewing the World boards, checking whether the boards are open, and the flight, route, map and weather requests above, do not ask.
  • The internet requests described above (the live feed, the flight route lookup, maps, weather, checking whether the boards are open, and viewing the World boards) still happen, so the services involved can see the device's IP address as described in "Internet requests the game makes". We don't keep it with anything about the child.

Players 13 and older. The full game is available once the player was born 14 or more calendar years ago, so they are at least 13. Those players can sign in to Google Play Games and post ranked scores, as described above.

We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, email thinaircapital@gmail.com and we will delete it. Parents and guardians can ask us, at the same address, what information we hold about their child.

Where your information is stored

Information we hold for the online boards is stored by Cloudflare, in the location assigned to our database when it was created. Our service providers (Cloudflare, Anthropic and Google) may process it elsewhere under their own safeguards.

Security

We send information over encrypted connections (HTTPS). No system is perfectly secure, so we also keep only what the game needs.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to ask what information we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, or to object to how it is used. To use these rights, email thinaircapital@gmail.com. We will respond within the time the law requires. We don't sell personal information, and we don't use it for targeted advertising.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version here with a new effective date. If the change is material, the game will show it to you and ask you to agree again.
