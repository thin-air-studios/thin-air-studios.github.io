If you want to direct planes to runways on your phone, you have more choice than you might expect. Some games are quick puzzles, some are built around a single airport, and a few put real flight data on the screen. This guide compares the options that we could check, with the strengths of each.

One disclosure first. Short Final is our game, made by Thin Air Studios. We include it in this list because it is part of the choice, and we describe it the same way we describe the others: what it does, what it does not do, and who it might suit. Where another game does something better, we say so.

## How we checked

We looked at the store pages and public listings for air traffic control games, and kept the ones that put you in charge of aircraft at an airport, rather than flying one yourself. The list is not complete. Stores change daily, and many small games come and go.

We did not use star ratings or download counts. We could not check those in a way we would stand behind, so this guide does not quote them. Read the current listing and recent reviews before you install anything, and check the app's permissions and any ads or purchases.

## The main options

**Planes Control (ATC), by Rarepixels.** This is an Android game that asks you to draw a landing path from each plane to a runway of the matching colour, in the spirit of the classic flight-control puzzle. According to its Google Play summary, as reproduced by PocketGamer, it has more than 60 planes and helicopters, and 13 levels on phones plus six on tablets. It suits players who want many aircraft types and a set of levels to work through. [PocketGamer: Planes Control on Google Play](https://www.pocketgamer.com/googleplay/com.rarepixels.planeslittle)

**Air Traffic Control 2D.** Its App Store listing describes a game in which you sketch routes with a finger to guide aircraft to their destinations. It lists more than 30 aircraft types, including helicopters and seaplanes, with real-city maps and some maps themed on the Second World War. The listing says it is ad-supported and includes in-app purchases. We could not confirm its Android listing in our search, so check the Google Play page before you look for it there. [Apple App Store: Air Traffic Control 2D](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id949855469)

**Air Control Lite.** This game is credited to Four Pixels Games. You tap an incoming aircraft and draw a path to the correct runway or helipad, and helicopters are directed to a helipad. We found it on third-party download sites, not a Google Play listing we could check. Install games only from the official store, and check the listing for the publisher and permissions first.

**Short Final, by Thin Air Studios (our game).** [Short Final](/) puts you in charge of traffic at one airport at a time, such as [Dallas/Fort Worth](/airports/kdfw/). Its distinctive feature is that the planes are real. When you pick an airport, real planes from live flight data arrive at the edge of the screen under their flight numbers, and you draw a path from each one to a runway. Simulated planes fill the sky to match your level, unless you turn on Real planes only, which uses nothing but the real planes.

Here is what the store listing says about Short Final today. It is free to download, with no ads and no account. The daily challenge is always free. After your first three days you get three free games a day at any airport, and a one-time Short Final Pro purchase in Google Play gives unlimited games. Radio chatter, which shows tower and pilot calls in air traffic phraseology, is an optional setting.

## Which one to pick

Pick by what you want from the game.

- **For variety**, choose a game with many aircraft types and a set of levels that get harder. Planes Control and Air Traffic Control 2D both list many types.
- **For real traffic**, choose a game that uses live data, and accept that the traffic will vary. Short Final is built this way, and its real-only setting keeps the game to real planes.
- **For a quick puzzle**, pick a short game with simple controls and a clear end, and expect fewer features.
- **For a serious simulation** of procedures, slots, flight planning and radar, look beyond phone games. A phone game shows the shape of the job, not its full detail.

Whichever you choose, check the price model before you start. Some games are free and show ads. Some are paid once. Some are free with purchases inside the game. Short Final's own price model is written out above, and the store will show you the others.

## What the games do not teach

Phone games are a good way to learn the shape of air traffic control: keep planes apart, get them to the right runway, and handle the unexpected. They are not a good way to learn the procedures. Real controllers use fixed phrases, follow published procedures, manage time slots, and work with radar and flight plans that no phone game shows in full.

If you want to learn the real words, read our guide to radio phraseology. If you want to understand how a real airport runs, read the guide on how air traffic control works. Both explain the pieces that a game can only suggest.

## Where Short Final fits

Short Final is best for players who want a short, focused game at a real airport, with real planes when they are in the air. It is less suited to players who want a long campaign of levels, a deep set of aircraft types or a full simulation of procedures. Its strengths are the real traffic, the maps for more than 6,000 airports built from OpenStreetMap, and the ability to pick almost any airport in the world.

We would rather you pick the game that suits you than the one we make. If you try Short Final, the browser demo lets you see the idea first, without installing anything.
